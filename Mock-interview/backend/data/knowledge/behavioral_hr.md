# Behavioral and HR Interview Guidance

## STAR method for behavioral questions
Answer questions such as "Tell me about a time when..." with STAR: Situation (brief context), Task (what you were responsible for), Action (what you personally did, step by step) and Result (the outcome, ideally with a measurable result and what you learned). Spend most of the time on Action and Result and use "I" for your own contribution.

## Tell me about yourself
Keep it to about 60 to 90 seconds in three parts: present (your degree, year and focus area), past (relevant projects, skills or internships that prepared you) and future (why you want this role). Stay professional and relevant; do not recite your whole resume or personal life story.

## Why do you want to join this company
Show you have done your homework: mention things you genuinely researched, such as the company's products or services, the role's responsibilities and the technologies it uses, and connect them to your skills and goals. Avoid generic flattery and do not claim facts about the company that you are not sure of.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Pick strengths relevant to the role and back each with a short example. For a weakness, name a real but non-critical one, and explain what you are doing to improve it. Avoid disguised strengths such as "I work too hard".

## How to explain a project
Structure it as: the problem it solves, your role and contribution, the technologies you chose and why, one real challenge and how you solved it, and the result or what you learned. Be ready to answer follow-up questions on every technology and line listed on your resume.

## Handling failure or conflict
Questions like "Tell me about a time you faced a conflict in your team" or "Tell me about a time you failed" test maturity. For a failure, own your part honestly, explain what you changed afterwards and what you learned. For a conflict, show that you listened to the other view, focused on the shared goal and the facts, and reached a constructive outcome rather than blaming a person.

## Teamwork and leadership examples
Good examples show a specific situation: how you divided work in a group project, helped a teammate who was stuck, took initiative, or handled a deadline. Mention your concrete actions and the result for the team.

## Where do you see yourself in five years
Show realistic ambition connected to the role: building strong technical depth, taking on more responsibility, and growing with the company. Avoid saying you plan to leave soon or giving an unrelated career plan.

## Verbal answer technique
Pause for a moment to structure your thoughts, give the direct answer first and then explain, keep each answer to roughly one or two minutes, speak clearly at a steady pace, and avoid filler words. If you do not know something, say so honestly and explain how you would find out or what related knowledge you have.

## Approach to a coding or debugging question
Restate the problem and clarify inputs and edge cases, work through a small example, describe a simple brute-force solution first, then improve it, state the time and space complexity, write clean code with meaningful names, and test it against edge cases such as empty input, a single element and duplicates. Talk through your thinking aloud.

## Being a fresher: handling gaps in knowledge
Be honest about what you have not used, then connect to what you do know, for example "I haven't used that tool in a project, but I have worked with a similar one and here is how I would learn it". Emphasise projects, learning speed and fundamentals.

## Questions to ask the interviewer
Prepare two or three thoughtful questions, such as what a typical first project for a new joiner looks like, what skills the team values most, or how performance and learning are supported. Avoid asking about salary or leave in a first technical round.
